What should I ask first about a painter's insurance?

Ask whether the painting company carries general liability insurance and workers' compensation coverage where required, then request current documentation. Ask the contractor to confirm that the policy applies to your project, the work being done, and any employees or subcontractors who will be on site.

A certificate of insurance is useful evidence, but it is not the policy itself and does not change policy terms. If the project is substantial or the paperwork is unclear, call the insurance agent or carrier listed on the certificate to verify that coverage is active. Get any project-specific promises in writing.

What should painting insurance cover?

General liability coverage may respond to certain claims involving accidental property damage or bodily injury connected with the contractor's work, subject to the policy's limits, exclusions, and terms. Workers' compensation is a separate coverage that may apply to job-related employee injuries. Ask the painter to explain what coverage they carry, rather than assuming the policy covers every possible loss.

Ask how the company handles subcontractors and whether they provide their own insurance documents. Also ask about exclusions that could matter for your home, such as damage to existing finishes, paint spills, overspray, or work involving repairs beyond painting. Coverage varies by policy, so ask the insurer or agent about wording you do not understand.

What should a local professional check before work starts?

A careful contractor should review the project scope, surfaces, access, and who will perform the work before confirming what documentation is relevant. For a Bonita Springs home, that could mean noting whether the job includes interior rooms, exterior walls, trim, pressure washing, repairs, or work around shared areas. The written proposal should make clear what the crew is responsible for and what is outside the scope.

Ask the company to match the name on its proposal to the name shown on the insurance certificate. Check the policy dates and ask whether coverage will remain active through the planned work period. If your property manager, lender, or association asks for particular wording or to be listed on a document, confirm with the insurer whether that can be provided.

What changes the cost of hiring an insured painter?

Insurance is only one part of a painting estimate, and there is no reliable way to compare bids by price alone. Cost can change with the area being painted, surface condition, preparation and repairs, paint and finish choices, access, number of coats, and how much protection or cleanup the property needs.

The proposed crew size, subcontracting, and scheduling can also affect how a contractor organizes the job. Ask whether the estimate includes surface preparation, materials, moving or covering items, cleanup, and touch-ups, and ask which conditions could lead to added work. A detailed scope lets you compare providers on the same basis.

What can affect the schedule?

Timing depends on the size of the project, repairs and preparation, crew availability, access to the property, and the time needed for coats to dry. Exterior work can also be affected by rain, humidity, and surface conditions, so ask the painter how they handle weather delays in Bonita Springs.

Insurance checks are best done before the start date, not on the morning the crew arrives. If a certificate needs to be updated or a property manager requires additional documentation, allow time for the agent or insurer to respond. Ask for the expected start window and how schedule changes will be communicated.

What insurance mistakes should you avoid?

  • Do not treat a certificate as a guarantee that every type of damage or injury is covered.
  • Do not assume a subcontractor is covered by the painting company's policy. Ask for documentation or written confirmation from the insurer.
  • Do not rely on an old certificate. Check the dates and verify active coverage for the planned work period.
  • Do not assume a business name on a proposal and a different name on a certificate refer to the same insured company. Ask the agent to clarify.
  • Do not skip the written scope. Clear responsibility for preparation, repairs, protection, and cleanup can help prevent disagreements.

For a property with an association or management company, ask about its requirements before signing. Rules may include specific insurance documentation or advance approval, and requirements can differ by property.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • Can you send a current certificate of insurance for the company doing the work?
  • Is general liability coverage active for the dates of my project, and who can verify it?
  • What workers' compensation coverage do you carry, where required, and how are any exemptions handled?
  • Will employees or subcontractors work at my property, and can you provide their insurance details?
  • Does the coverage apply to the specific work in my proposal, including preparation and repairs?
  • What exclusions or limits should I understand before work begins?
  • Can you provide any insurance wording my property manager or association requires?
  • What is included in the written estimate, and how will changes or accidental damage be handled?
