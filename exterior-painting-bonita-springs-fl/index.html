What should exterior painting include?

A well-planned exterior painting job covers more than applying paint. It should begin with a review of the surfaces and their condition, followed by a written scope that identifies which areas will be painted, what preparation is included, and what coating system the painter recommends.

Depending on the home, preparation may include washing, removing loose paint, sanding rough edges, caulking suitable joints, and priming bare or repaired areas. The crew should also protect windows, doors, plants, walkways, and other surfaces that are not part of the work. Ask which steps apply to your home, since stucco, wood, masonry, and metal do not all need the same treatment.

Before work starts, confirm the included surfaces, number of coats, products, cleanup plan, and how changes or repairs will be handled. A clear scope helps you compare estimates on the work itself, not just the final total.

What does a local professional check first?

Weather and exposure can affect how an exterior holds up, so ask the painter to assess your home's conditions. A painter should look for peeling or chalking paint, cracks, stains, soft or damaged wood, gaps around trim, and areas where water may be getting behind the coating. These are clues to investigate, not issues that should automatically be covered with another coat.

  • Which materials and surfaces are included, and whether previous coatings are sound.
  • Where paint is loose, faded, blistered, or showing signs of poor adhesion.
  • Whether cracks, failed caulk, or damaged trim need attention before painting.
  • How the crew will keep rain, direct sun, and damp surfaces from interfering with application.
  • Whether plants, outdoor fixtures, screens, and nearby property need extra protection.

A good walkthrough ends with a practical plan. If a contractor sees a potential moisture or repair issue, ask what they observed and whether the painting estimate includes the investigation or repair.

Which preparation and coating choices matter?

The right product depends on the surface, its current condition, and the exposure it gets. A coating suitable for stucco may not be the right choice for metal railings or wood trim. Ask the painter to identify the proposed primer and finish products and explain why they fit the materials on your home.

Preparation is just as important as product selection. Painting over dirt, loose paint, failed caulk, or damp areas can lead to early peeling or uneven results. Get clear about whether cleaning, scraping, sanding, caulking, spot priming, and minor repairs are included, and ask the contractor to note any exclusions.

Color can also affect planning. A major change in shade may require a different primer or additional coverage, so discuss the color choice and expected coats before approving the scope. If your home is part of an association, check its color approval process before ordering paint.

What changes the cost of exterior painting?

There is no reliable price to give without seeing the home and defining the work. The estimate can change with the size and layout of the property, the number and type of surfaces, access to upper areas, and how much preparation or repair is needed.

The coating system, color change, number of coats, and amount of masking or protection can also affect the total. Ask whether the estimate includes areas such as trim, doors, soffits, railings, or detached structures, and whether repairs beyond the stated scope will require approval and a separate charge.

When you compare estimates, look for the same surfaces, preparation steps, products, coat expectations, and cleanup. If one proposal is much lower, ask what is different rather than assuming the two bids cover identical work.

What can affect the schedule?

Timing depends on the size of the job, surface condition, repair needs, crew availability, and how much drying time the products require. Weather and surface conditions can affect when paint can be applied, so a responsible schedule may include some flexibility.

Ask how the painter will communicate weather delays and whether the crew expects to work on all elevations at once or in stages. Before work begins, move or identify items that need access, and agree on how the crew will handle gates, outdoor furniture, and areas used by your household.

Questions to ask before you hire: exterior painting checklist

  • Which exterior surfaces are included in this estimate?
  • What cleaning, scraping, sanding, caulking, and priming will you do?
  • What paint and primer do you recommend for each surface, and why?
  • How many coats are included, and how will you handle a major color change?
  • Are repairs included, and how will you get my approval for work outside the scope?
  • How will you protect plants, windows, walkways, and outdoor fixtures?
  • Who will supervise the job, and how will weather delays or schedule changes be shared?
  • What cleanup is included, and what should I review with you at the final walkthrough?

Ask for the answers in the proposal or another written project document. A clear record makes it easier to resolve differences about what was agreed to.

Which mistakes should you avoid?

  • Choosing a provider based only on the lowest total without comparing the scope.
  • Treating peeling paint or recurring stains as a cosmetic problem without asking about the cause.
  • Assuming all surfaces, repairs, and trim are included in a general exterior painting estimate.
  • Approving a color before checking any association requirements that apply to your property.
  • Starting without agreeing on weather delays, access, protection, and cleanup.

Call a professional when paint is failing across several areas, when wood feels soft, when cracks keep returning, or when you suspect moisture behind the surface. A painter can explain what they see and whether the condition needs further assessment before coating begins.
