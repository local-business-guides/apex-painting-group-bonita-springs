What should interior painting include?

A good interior painting scope spells out the rooms and surfaces to be painted, the paint and finish, the preparation, and how the crew will protect the home. It should also say what is not included, such as major drywall repairs, moving heavy furniture, or painting inside closets.

For many repainting projects, preparation may involve protecting floors and furnishings, removing or covering outlet plates, cleaning surfaces, sanding rough spots, filling small holes, and priming repairs. The painter should explain which of those steps apply to your walls, trim, doors, or ceilings rather than treating every surface the same.

Before work starts, agree on the colors, sheen, number of coats in the scope, access to the rooms, and how touch-ups will be handled. Get those details in writing so there is a shared understanding of the finished job.

What does a local professional check before estimating?

A useful walkthrough looks beyond room size. The painter should note the condition of the walls, existing paint, stains, cracks, peeling areas, trim, and any surfaces that may need more preparation or a different primer.

In Bonita Springs, a professional should also look for signs of moisture or mildew and ask about leaks or past water damage before painting over a problem area. Paint can improve the appearance of a surface, but it does not fix the cause of moisture, so that issue may need attention first.

Expect questions about furniture, pets, building access, occupied rooms, and your preferred schedule. If the home is in a community with color or work rules, mention those early so you can check them before the project is booked.

What changes the cost of interior painting?

The price depends on the amount of work, not only the floor area of a home. Room count, wall height, ceilings, trim, doors, closets, and the number of colors all affect how much labor and paint a project calls for.

Surface condition is another major factor. Peeling paint, stains, damaged drywall, heavy texture, or a large color change can require extra preparation, repairs, or primer. Ask the estimator to separate ordinary prep from repairs that may need a different trade or a revised scope.

Paint selection, access, occupied rooms, furniture handling, and the requested schedule can also affect the estimate. Get a written proposal that identifies the surfaces, preparation, products or product allowances, coats, exclusions, and any conditions that could change the quoted work.

How long might the project take?

Timing depends on the number and size of rooms, how much preparation is needed, drying time, and whether the home is occupied. Painting a few clear rooms is different from working around furniture throughout a home or repairing damaged surfaces before applying paint.

Ask for the expected start date, the likely work sequence, and how the crew will handle rooms that need to remain usable. Confirm whether the schedule includes time for repairs, drying, and a final walkthrough, rather than assuming every step happens in one visit.

If your move, guests, or other renovation work sets a deadline, say so before accepting an estimate. A professional should explain what can reasonably be completed and flag any factors that could affect the timing.

What mistakes should you avoid when comparing estimates?

  • Choosing on price alone without checking whether the estimates cover the same rooms, surfaces, preparation, and materials.
  • Assuming that a verbal promise includes patching, priming, trim, ceilings, or moving furniture. Ask for each item to be listed.
  • Leaving color and sheen decisions until the crew is ready to paint. Confirm selections and where each color goes in advance.
  • Painting over signs of a leak, persistent moisture, or mildew without addressing the underlying issue.
  • Not asking who will protect floors and furnishings, clean up, and review the completed work with you.

A short written scope can prevent a lot of confusion. If two proposals differ, ask each provider to explain the difference before you decide.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • Which rooms and surfaces are included in this estimate?
  • What preparation and minor repairs are included, and what would cost extra?
  • What paint and sheen are you proposing for each surface?
  • How will floors, furniture, fixtures, and nearby rooms be protected?
  • Who will be doing the work, and who should I contact with questions?
  • What schedule do you expect, including preparation and drying time?
  • How do you handle a change in scope or an issue found after work begins?
  • Will we have a final walkthrough, and what is the process for requesting touch-ups?

When should you call a professional?

Consider hiring a professional when the job involves multiple rooms, high ceilings, detailed trim, extensive surface repairs, or a schedule that makes a long project difficult to manage. A painter can also help you understand whether a surface needs additional preparation before it is ready for a new finish.

Get advice before painting if you see active leaks, recurring mildew, peeling over a large area, or damage that may extend beneath the surface. Those conditions may call for repair or moisture investigation first, rather than a fresh coat of paint.
