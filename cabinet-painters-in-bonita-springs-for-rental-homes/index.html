What should cabinet painting for a rental home include?

A complete cabinet painting job is more than putting a new coat of paint on visible doors. The painter should inspect the cabinet boxes and doors, identify the surface material, discuss repairs, and explain the preparation and finish planned for the job.

The work commonly involves removing and labeling doors and hardware, cleaning away grease, sanding or otherwise preparing the surface, applying a suitable primer, and using a cabinet finish. Ask whether drawer fronts, cabinet interiors, end panels, and hardware are included, since the scope can vary. The painter should also explain drying and curing time before the cabinets are used normally.

What will a local painter check before recommending a finish?

A careful assessment starts with the condition of the existing finish. Peeling, water damage, swollen edges, loose veneer, and old repairs may need attention before paint can hold well. The painter should also check whether the doors are wood, previously painted material, laminate, or another surface, because preparation and product choices depend on what is there.

In a rental, the painter should ask how the home is used and when the next tenant is expected. A hard-wearing finish and a realistic cure period matter, especially if appliances, cleaners, or move-in activity could bump or mark the cabinets soon after painting. Share any known issues and the turnover date early, rather than planning around the shortest possible work window.

What changes the cost and timing?

The price depends on the size and condition of the cabinets, how much preparation or repair they need, the number of doors and drawers, and the finish selected. The scope also matters: painting cabinet boxes, interiors, panels, or trim can add work compared with painting doors and drawer fronts only. Hardware replacement or extra removal and reinstallation may affect the estimate too.

Timing depends on the same condition and scope, along with how the painter stages the doors and allows for drying and curing. A schedule that includes preparation, finish coats, reinstallation, and a final check is more useful than a promise based only on how quickly paint can be applied. Ask for the expected start and finish dates and what could delay them.

How should you plan cabinet work around a rental turnover?

Schedule an assessment as soon as you know the unit will be vacant, and share the date the home needs to be ready. Cabinet work may need to happen alongside cleaning, flooring, appliance servicing, or other repairs, so agree on the order before trades arrive. Keep the kitchen clear and confirm who is responsible for moving items and protecting nearby surfaces.

If the home will be occupied during the work, discuss access, ventilation, noise, and how the kitchen will remain usable. For a property in a condominium or community with its own rules, check any applicable work-hour, access, or contractor requirements with the association or property manager. Requirements can vary, so don't assume a vacant unit is exempt.

What mistakes should rental owners avoid?

  • Choosing a quote without checking whether it includes thorough cleaning, surface preparation, primer, and finish coats.
  • Assuming every cabinet material can be painted the same way, especially when a surface has peeling edges or prior coating problems.
  • Booking the work so close to move-in that there is no time for curing, touch-ups, or a final inspection.
  • Leaving the painter to guess which doors belong where. Ask how doors and hardware will be labeled and protected.
  • Treating a low quote as a complete comparison without checking repairs, materials, reinstallation, and cleanup.

These details matter in a rental because a rushed or incomplete finish can create extra work between tenants. A written scope helps both sides spot missing tasks before the job starts.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • What cabinet materials and existing finish conditions have you identified?
  • Which preparation steps, repairs, primer, and finish coats are included?
  • Are cabinet boxes, doors, drawer fronts, interiors, and end panels all part of the scope?
  • Will you remove and label doors and hardware, then reinstall them after finishing?
  • What is the expected schedule, including drying and curing time?
  • How will the work area, counters, floors, and nearby appliances be protected?
  • What is excluded from the written estimate, and how are additional repairs approved?
  • Can the work be coordinated with our turnover date, access arrangements, or community rules?

When should you call a cabinet painter, and what is the next step?

Call a professional when the finish is peeling, the surface material is uncertain, repairs may be needed, or the home has a fixed move-in date. A painter can assess whether the cabinets are a good candidate for refinishing and outline the preparation and cure time required.
