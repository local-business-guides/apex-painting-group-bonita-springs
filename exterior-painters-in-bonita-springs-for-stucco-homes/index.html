What should exterior painting for a stucco home include?

A stucco repaint should start with an assessment, not a quick estimate based only on the home's visible square footage. The painter should look at the finish, existing coating, cracks, peeling areas, chalking, and any signs that moisture may be getting behind or through the stucco.

A complete scope commonly describes cleaning, surface repairs, protection for windows and landscaping, priming where appropriate, and application of the specified exterior coating. It should also state which surfaces are included, how many coats are planned, and how the crew will handle areas that need extra preparation. Ask for those details in writing so you can compare proposals fairly.

What will a local painter check before recommending a coating?

Stucco can have different textures and finishes, and a coating that suits one surface may not be right for another. A professional should check whether the existing paint is sound, whether the surface feels powdery, and whether cracks appear to be hairline or wider. Those findings help determine the preparation and product approach.

  • Cracks, loose stucco, peeling paint, or spots that need repair before coating.
  • Chalking or a dusty surface that could interfere with adhesion.
  • Areas near windows, roof edges, doors, and penetrations where water may enter.
  • Shade and exposure, since strong sun and damp areas can affect how a coating performs and how quickly it dries.
  • Nearby surfaces and landscaping that need covering during cleaning, repairs, and painting.

Painting can improve appearance, but it does not fix a leak or a serious stucco problem. If the inspection raises concern about active water entry, ask what repair is needed and who should handle it before painting begins.

What changes the cost of painting a stucco home?

The price depends on the condition and complexity of the home, not just its floor plan. A larger exterior, multiple stories, difficult access, detailed trim, and many doors or windows can add labor and protection work. A proposal may also change if the stucco needs repairs, failing paint must be removed, or extra surface preparation is required.

The coating and number of coats specified matter too. Ask whether the proposal identifies the product, covers the same surfaces you asked about, and includes cleanup and protection. If a quote is notably lower than others, check whether it leaves out repairs, primer, or a second coat rather than assuming the difference is a bargain.

How can you compare the schedule and timing?

Project timing depends on the amount of preparation, repair needs, crew availability, and the time required for products to dry between steps. Weather can affect washing, repairs, and coating application, so a responsible estimate may give a working schedule rather than a guaranteed finish date.

Ask when the crew expects to start, how many workdays are anticipated, and how you'll be told about weather delays. Before work begins, confirm what needs to be moved or kept clear and whether access to doors, walkways, and outdoor areas will be affected.

What mistakes should homeowners avoid?

  • Choosing a quote by the total alone without checking the preparation and repair details.
  • Painting over loose, powdery, or damp areas without addressing the surface problem.
  • Assuming every crack is cosmetic or that paint will stop water from entering.
  • Failing to confirm which walls, trim, doors, and other exterior features are included.
  • Skipping a written agreement on the coating, coats, cleanup, and how additional repairs will be approved.

If the stucco has extensive damage, recurring damp spots, or cracks that appear to be growing, ask for an assessment before scheduling a paint job. A painter can point out concerns, but some conditions may call for a stucco or moisture specialist first.

Questions to ask checklist before you hire

  • What stucco or paint conditions did you find during the inspection?
  • Which cleaning, repairs, and surface preparation are included in the written proposal?
  • What coating and number of coats do you recommend, and why?
  • Which walls, trim, doors, and other exterior surfaces are included or excluded?
  • Who will complete the work, and who is my main contact during the project?
  • What schedule do you expect, and how do weather delays affect it?
  • How will you protect windows, plants, walkways, and nearby surfaces?
  • How will added repairs be documented and approved before the work continues?

When should you call a professional, and what is the next step?

Call a professional if the exterior has peeling or chalking paint, cracks you cannot assess, hard-to-reach areas, or signs of moisture. A site visit can help separate ordinary preparation from repairs that should happen before painting. If there is active water entry or damaged stucco, ask about that condition before approving a coating project.
