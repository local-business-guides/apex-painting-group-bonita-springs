What should a house painting service include?

A good painting project starts with a clear inspection and scope, not just a color selection. The estimate should identify the rooms or exterior surfaces to be painted, the preparation work, the paint and finish, the number of coats, and what cleanup is included.

Preparation may include protecting floors, furniture, landscaping, and nearby fixtures; cleaning surfaces; scraping loose paint; sanding; filling small defects; and priming where needed. Larger repairs, water damage, or wood replacement should be called out separately so there is no confusion about what the painting work covers.

What should a local painter check at the home?

In Bonita Springs, a painter should look closely at how sun, moisture, and outdoor exposure have affected the surfaces. On an exterior, that can mean checking for chalking, peeling, mildew, cracked caulk, and areas where water may be getting behind the finish. Stucco, siding, trim, and metal can each need a different preparation plan.

For interior work, the walkthrough should include wall and ceiling condition, stains, previous repairs, and areas that need extra protection. Ask whether a stain or moisture issue needs attention before painting. Fresh paint can cover a mark, but it will not fix the cause of a leak or a failing surface.

A useful estimate reflects what the painter actually saw at the home. If a proposal is prepared without a meaningful walkthrough, ask how the company has accounted for repairs, access, and surface condition.

What affects the cost of house painting?

The price depends on the amount of surface area, its condition, and how much preparation it needs. Peeling paint, cracks, stains, heavy mildew, damaged trim, and difficult access can add work. A home with sound walls and a straightforward color change is different from one that needs extensive repairs or careful protection.

The estimate can also change with the number of rooms or surfaces, ceiling height, the paint and finish selected, and whether doors, cabinets, trim, or multiple colors are included. Exterior work may require more setup around landscaping, walkways, and outdoor fixtures. Ask for a written scope that separates painting from repairs that are outside the proposal.

There is no useful way to compare prices unless the bids cover the same work. If one estimate is much lower, ask what is excluded and how many coats and which preparation steps are included before treating it as a direct comparison.

How long can a painting project take?

Timing depends on the size of the job, the condition of the surfaces, the number of coats, and how many people are assigned to the work. Repairs and drying time can add days or change the sequence, while exterior work may need to pause when rain or damp conditions interfere with preparation or application.

Ask the painter for an expected start window, an estimated duration, and how schedule changes will be communicated. Before the crew arrives, confirm what needs to be moved and whether rooms or outdoor areas will be unavailable during particular stages.

What mistakes should homeowners avoid?

  • Choosing from the total alone without checking the work and materials included.
  • Assuming damaged surfaces, water stains, or wood repairs are covered when they are not listed.
  • Skipping questions about who will protect the home and handle daily cleanup.
  • Approving a color or finish without checking how it looks in the home's lighting.
  • Leaving access, furniture, pets, or landscaping arrangements until the crew is ready to start.

A written scope and a short pre-start conversation prevent many avoidable surprises. If you see new damage or a change in the work after the estimate, ask for the added task and any cost or schedule effect to be documented before it proceeds.

What questions should you ask before you hire?

  • Which rooms, walls, or exterior surfaces are included in this estimate?
  • What preparation and priming do you expect these surfaces will need?
  • How many coats are included, and what paint and finish are proposed?
  • Are repairs, stain treatment, and replacement of damaged materials included or excluded?
  • How will floors, furnishings, landscaping, and fixtures be protected?
  • Who will be on site, and who should I contact if the scope changes?
  • What is the expected start window and project duration, and how will weather delays be handled?
  • What cleanup and final walkthrough are included before the job is considered complete?

Before you book: a painting checklist

  • Confirm the estimate lists every room or exterior surface you want painted.
  • Check that preparation, primer, paint, finish, and number of coats are written down.
  • Clarify which repairs are included and which will cost extra.
  • Agree on how furniture, floors, landscaping, and fixtures will be protected.
  • Confirm the expected start window, project duration, cleanup, and final walkthrough.

What is the next step?
