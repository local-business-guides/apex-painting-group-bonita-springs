What should a painting service include?

A clear painting proposal should spell out the rooms or exterior surfaces included, the preparation planned, the paint and finish being considered, and how many coats are expected. It should also explain what happens to furniture, floors, fixtures, plants, and other nearby areas while work is underway.

Preparation can include cleaning, filling small holes, sanding rough spots, removing loose paint, caulking gaps, and priming where needed. Not every surface needs the same treatment, so ask the painter to identify what is included and what would count as additional repair work. Cleanup and a final walkthrough belong in the conversation too.

What does a local professional check before estimating?

A careful estimate starts with the actual surfaces, not just a room count or a quick look from the driveway. For interior work, a painter may check wall condition, previous coatings, ceiling height, trim details, access, and whether rooms will be occupied during the job. For exterior work, the condition of siding, stucco, trim, and exposed wood can change the preparation plan.

In Bonita Springs, sun, humidity, rain, and salty air can affect exterior coatings and the time available for outdoor work. Ask how the proposed paint is suited to the surface and exposure, and how the crew will decide if weather is right for application. A good estimate notes visible concerns rather than assuming every wall is ready for paint.

If the property is a condo or part of a managed community, access rules, work hours, elevator reservations, and approved colors may matter as much as the painting itself. Bring those requirements up during the estimate so the scope and schedule account for them.

What drives the cost of painting?

There isn't one useful price for every Bonita Springs painting job. The amount of surface area is only part of the estimate. Surface condition, repairs, access, detail work, and the time needed to protect and prepare the property can all affect the total.

  • Preparation, including scraping, sanding, filling, caulking, and priming.
  • Surface and layout, such as textured walls, high ceilings, detailed trim, or hard-to-reach areas.
  • Materials, including the coating and finish selected for the surface and its exposure.
  • Protection and access, especially in furnished homes, occupied condos, or shared buildings.
  • Scope changes, such as adding rooms, changing colors, or discovering damage after work starts.

Ask for a written estimate that separates included work from optional repairs or items that could not be confirmed during the walkthrough. If estimates differ, compare what each one says about preparation and coats before comparing only the bottom line.

What can change the schedule?

Timing depends on the size of the project, the condition of the surfaces, the number of colors and finishes, and how much preparation or repair is needed. Furniture, residents, pets, building access, and other trades can also affect how much work can happen each day.

Exterior painting needs suitable weather and enough time for coatings to dry between steps. In Southwest Florida, rain and humidity may alter the work plan, so ask how weather delays are handled and what the crew will do if a scheduled day isn't suitable. Get an expected start window and a realistic sequence of work rather than relying on a promise that ignores site conditions.

What mistakes should you avoid when hiring?

  • Choosing only by the lowest total without checking preparation, materials, and cleanup.
  • Assuming verbal details are part of the job when they aren't written into the estimate.
  • Overlooking condo association or property manager rules about colors, work hours, parking, or access.
  • Failing to agree on who moves furniture, removes wall items, or protects landscaping.
  • Approving extra work without first asking what it changes in the scope, cost, and schedule.

Before work begins, walk through the estimate with the person responsible for the job. Point out stains, peeling areas, water marks, or previous repairs, and ask how each will be handled. Painting can improve appearance, but it shouldn't be treated as a substitute for resolving an active leak or another underlying problem.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • Which rooms or exterior surfaces are included, and which are excluded?
  • What preparation and repairs are included before painting?
  • What paint and finish do you propose for these particular surfaces, and why?
  • How many coats are planned, and what would lead to a change?
  • Who will protect the property, move items, and handle cleanup?
  • Who is the main contact during the project, and how will changes be approved?
  • What start window and work sequence should I expect, including possible weather delays?
  • What should I confirm with my condo association, property manager, or local authorities before work starts?

Look for answers that are specific to the property and consistent with the written estimate. If a provider can't explain what happens when hidden damage or a change in scope comes up, clarify that before signing.

What local rules should you confirm?

For a condo, rental, or community with shared standards, check whether colors need approval and whether the building sets limits on work hours, contractor access, parking, or elevator use. Rules can vary by property, so confirm them with the association or manager instead of assuming a painter will know them.

For a standalone home, ask the relevant local office or property authority whether your particular project has requirements, especially if painting is part of a larger repair or exterior change. Ordinary painting and work involving repairs or structural changes may be treated differently. Confirm the details for your address and scope before scheduling.

What is the next step?

Make a short list of the surfaces you want painted, note any visible damage, and gather condo or property rules if they apply. Then request a walkthrough and written scope from the companies you're considering.
