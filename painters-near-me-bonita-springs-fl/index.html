What should a painting service include?

A reliable painting service starts with a clear description of the areas to be painted and the condition of each surface. The estimate should spell out preparation, paint and finish, protection for nearby areas, cleanup, and any work that is excluded.

Preparation can include washing, scraping loose or failing paint, sanding, patching, caulking, and priming where appropriate. The right steps depend on the surface and its condition, so a proposal that simply says “paint the house” may leave too much room for misunderstanding.

Ask how the crew will handle furniture, landscaping, fixtures, and access to the work area. For exterior work, clarify which sides or structures are included and how the schedule may change if weather interrupts the job.

What does a local professional check before quoting?

A useful estimate is based on seeing the surfaces, not just guessing from a few broad measurements. A painter may look for peeling or chalking paint, cracks, stains, damaged trim, uneven patches, and places where old coatings may not hold well.

In Bonita Springs, sun, humidity, rain, and coastal exposure can affect how exterior surfaces age. The professional should consider the material and current condition before recommending preparation and products, rather than treating every wall, door, or trim surface alike.

  • Which rooms, walls, ceilings, doors, trim, or exterior sections are included?
  • Are there repairs or stains that need separate attention before painting?
  • Will the surface need special cleaning, primer, or a particular finish?
  • Are there access limits, pets, tenants, or business hours to plan around?

What changes the cost and timing?

There is no useful single price for every painting job. The total depends on the amount of surface area, how much preparation or repair is needed, the number of coats, the paint and finish selected, and how easy it is to reach the work area.

A room with clear walls and straightforward access is different from a project with peeling paint, stained surfaces, detailed trim, high ceilings, or several colors. Exterior work can also require scheduling around rain and suitable conditions for application and drying. Ask the contractor to explain which assumptions the estimate uses.

Timing can change with the scope, crew availability, drying time, weather, and any repairs discovered after work begins. Get an expected start window and a realistic duration, then ask how the contractor will communicate a delay or a change in the work.

What mistakes should you avoid when hiring?

  • Choosing by the bottom-line amount without checking what work and materials are included.
  • Assuming surface repairs, primer, or extra coats are included when the estimate does not say so.
  • Leaving color, sheen, or product selections until the crew is ready to start.
  • Not agreeing on how furniture, landscaping, fixtures, and completed areas will be protected.
  • Relying on a verbal promise instead of keeping the scope and any changes in writing.

A quick comparison table can help: list each provider’s included areas, preparation, products, cleanup, schedule, and exclusions. If two estimates differ sharply, ask what explains the difference instead of assuming one is automatically the better value.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • Which exact rooms, surfaces, or exterior areas are included in the written estimate?
  • What preparation and repairs are included, and what would cost extra?
  • What paint type and finish do you recommend for these surfaces, and why?
  • Who will be on site, and who should I contact with questions?
  • How will floors, furniture, plants, and nearby fixtures be protected?
  • What start window and work duration should I plan for?
  • How do you document and price any change in scope?
  • What cleanup and review of the finished work are included?

A professional should be able to answer these in plain language and put the agreed details in writing. If an answer is vague, ask for clarification before signing or scheduling.

Do local rules affect a painting project?

A straightforward repaint may have different requirements from work that changes building features or involves repairs, and rules can vary by property and location. If the home is in an association or managed community, check its color approval and scheduling process before ordering paint or setting a start date.

For questions about permits or local requirements, confirm with the relevant city or county office and your association, if applicable. Ask the contractor to identify any issue they believe applies, but verify requirements with the authority responsible for the property.

What is the next step?

Make a short list of the areas you want painted, note any visible damage, and gather color or finish ideas. Then arrange a walkthrough with one or more providers and request written estimates based on the same scope.
