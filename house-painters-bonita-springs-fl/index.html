What should house painting include?

A clear painting proposal should describe the rooms or exterior surfaces being painted, the preparation each surface needs, the paint and finish, and how many coats are planned. It should also spell out what is not included, so you know whether repairs, special primers, or moving heavy furniture are part of the work.

A typical plan may include protecting floors, furniture, landscaping, and nearby fixtures; cleaning surfaces; removing loose paint; sanding or patching minor flaws; priming where needed; and applying the agreed paint. Cleanup and a final walkthrough should be discussed before the crew starts, not left as an assumption.

What does a local painter check before quoting?

A painter should look at the condition and material of the surfaces, not just estimate by room count or the front of the house. Peeling paint, water marks, cracks, mildew, chalking, and earlier patch jobs can all call for different preparation. If a problem looks like an active leak or a larger repair, painting alone may not address it.

  • Which surfaces need cleaning, scraping, sanding, patching, or primer?
  • Are there stains, gaps, or damaged areas that need attention before paint goes on?
  • What finish and color are planned, and will the existing color affect coverage?
  • How will the crew protect nearby rooms, plants, fixtures, and walkways?

For exterior work, ask how the painter will account for sun exposure, rain, humidity, and the condition of trim, siding, or stucco. In Bonita Springs, the schedule should leave room for suitable weather and dry time rather than treating every day as a paint day.

How do you choose between interior and exterior painting?

Interior painting can be a good fit when walls, ceilings, doors, or trim look worn, or when a room's color is changing. The plan should account for furniture, access, ventilation, pets, and how the household will use the rooms while work is underway.

Exterior painting focuses on the home's outside surfaces and their exposure to weather. Ask whether the estimate covers the full exterior or only named areas, such as trim, entry doors, or a section of siding. What affects the cost of house painting?

The price depends on the size and layout of the area, how much surface preparation is needed, the number of colors, and the type and condition of the surfaces. High ceilings, detailed trim, hard-to-reach areas, extensive patching, and a major color change can add labor or materials. Exterior access and the condition of the existing coating also affect the scope.

Materials and finish choices matter, too. Ask whether the proposal names the paint product and finish, how many coats are included, and what happens if additional repairs are found after work begins. How long can the project take?

Timing varies with the amount of work, the number of painters, preparation needs, and how many rooms or surfaces have to be kept clear. Drying time between coats and suitable weather for exterior work can also affect the schedule. Ask for an expected start window, the likely work sequence, and how weather or a newly discovered repair could change the plan.

Before interior work, agree on access, furniture handling, and which rooms will be unavailable. For an exterior project, ask how the crew will protect landscaping and keep walkways usable. A realistic schedule should explain likely interruptions rather than promise a fixed finish date without conditions.

What mistakes should you avoid before hiring?

  • Choosing from the total price alone without comparing what preparation and materials are included.
  • Assuming every wall, ceiling, closet, door, or trim surface is covered by a general phrase like “interior painting.”
  • Skipping written details about coats, paint finish, cleanup, repairs, and exclusions.
  • Approving colors without checking samples in the home's lighting and beside existing finishes.
  • Forgetting to check whether an HOA, condominium association, or other community rules apply to exterior colors or work hours.

Rules differ by community, so check with the relevant association or property manager before exterior work begins. Do not rely on a painter's general impression as confirmation that a color or schedule has been approved.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • Which rooms or exterior surfaces are included, and what is excluded?
  • What preparation will you do for peeling paint, cracks, stains, or rough areas?
  • What paint product, finish, and number of coats are included?
  • Who will supervise the work and be my contact during the project?
  • How will you protect floors, furniture, landscaping, and fixtures?
  • What is the expected schedule, and what could delay it?
  • How will added repairs or a change in scope be approved and documented?
  • What should I check during the final walkthrough?

Clear answers help you compare proposals on the work itself, not just the bottom line. If a detail matters to you, ask to have it written into the scope.

When should you call a professional?

Call a professional when paint is peeling across several areas, surfaces need substantial preparation, the job involves multiple rooms or elevations, or you want a consistent finish across detailed trim and walls. A pro can also help identify when a stain, crack, or damaged area needs attention before painting. If you suspect an ongoing moisture problem, have that investigated before covering it with paint.
